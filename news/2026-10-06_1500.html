B-1AIエージェントに「活動ログ」を書かせろ——自律と放置を分けるたった1行の設計原則

📰 本文 + 和越対訳

Zenn作者のkokotanは、AIエージェントを本番環境で活用する際の信頼構築手法として「活動ログ」の重要性を提唱している。提案するのはJSONL形式のログシステムで、エージェントが実行したすべてのアクションを「何をしたか・目的・方法・結果・実行できなかった場合の理由」の5フィールドで記録する仕組みだ。著者が指摘する核心は逆説的なものだ——「確認できる状態を作ると、確認しなくてよくなる」。監査可能性があるだけで人間の心理的コストが下がり、エージェントへの委任が促進される。記事では人間の役割を「考える・認証する・決める・受け取る」の4つに絞り、意思決定と認証フローはエージェントに渡してはならないという明確な境界を示している。実装はPowerShellスクリプトで十分との立場で、過度な設計より動くものを優先する実用主義を貫いている。自律エージェントを放置せず信頼できるチームメンバーとして扱うための、シンプルで再現可能な設計パターンとして評価されている。

Tác giả kokotatan trên Zenn đề xuất "activity log" như nguyên tắc thiết kế cốt lõi để xây dựng niềm tin khi triển khai AI agent trong môi trường production. Hệ thống log dạng JSONL ghi lại mọi hành động của agent với 5 trường: đã làm gì, mục đích, phương pháp, kết quả, và lý do nếu không thực thi được. Điểm mấu chốt có tính nghịch lý: "tạo ra trạng thái có thể kiểm tra thì sẽ không cần phải kiểm tra nữa" — khả năng audit làm giảm chi phí tâm lý của con người và thúc đẩy việc ủy thác cho agent. Bài viết phân định rõ vai trò của con người chỉ còn 4 việc: suy nghĩ, xác thực, quyết định và nhận kết quả — không ủy thác luồng quyết định và xác thực cho agent. Cách tiếp cận thực dụng này, triển khai bằng PowerShell đơn giản, đang được cộng đồng agentic engineering đánh giá cao.

C-2Dust: Training Transformers Without Backpropagation — Zeroth-Order Method Reaches Competitive Scale for the First Time

📰 Article + Impact

QLabs Research has published Dust, a novel zeroth-order optimization method that pretrains transformer language models without using backpropagation — the first such approach shown competitive with gradient-based training at meaningful scale. Instead of computing gradients through backward passes, Dust perturbs activations independently at each token position, creating a "virtual population" where thousands of population members are evaluated in a single forward pass. This approach is orders of magnitude more efficient than previous weight-space evolutionary strategies like EGGROLL. The key finding is counterintuitive: larger models are more population-efficient than smaller ones. At 10M training tokens, a 243M-parameter model outperforms a 120× smaller model across nearly all population size configurations. Dust's gradient estimates also align increasingly with backprop gradients as population size grows, following a mathematically predictable law that holds from small to 1B token pretraining runs. While current computational overhead prevents practical replacement of backpropagation today, this work challenges the assumption that gradient-based training is a necessary requirement at scale — opening pathways for non-differentiable architectures, alternative optimization landscapes, and hardware designs that don't require gradient accumulation.

C-3Google Donates gVisor Sandbox Runtime to CNCF — Aims to Widen Adoption for AI Agent Workload Isolation

📰 Article + Impact

Google has announced the donation of gVisor — its sandboxed container runtime — to the Cloud Native Computing Foundation (CNCF). gVisor is an OCI-compliant runtime written in Go that provides significantly stronger workload isolation than standard containers by intercepting system calls inside a user-space guest kernel. Rather than sharing the host kernel directly, gVisor's "Sentry" component acts as a virtual kernel, intercepting and mediating all syscalls from containerized applications. Google cites three friction points motivating the donation: difficulty explaining gVisor's value proposition to potential adopters, repeated rejections of upstream Linux kernel patches needed for full functionality, and difficulty shipping macOS support due to internal organizational constraints. By transferring gVisor to the CNCF, Google hopes the independent governance model will accelerate adoption, upstream patch acceptance, and cross-platform development. The timing is significant: with AI agents increasingly executing untrusted code in production environments — from code interpreters to MCP servers — demand for sub-VM isolation boundaries is growing rapidly. gVisor is already used internally at Google to sandbox AI workloads and is gaining traction as a sandboxing primitive for agent runtimes.
