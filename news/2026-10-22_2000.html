C1Anthropic Strengthens Defenses Against Industrial-Scale Model Distillation Attacks

📰 Article (English)

Anthropic published a detailed post on October 12 describing new defenses against what it calls "distillation attacks" — covert, industrial-scale campaigns to extract Claude's capabilities and replicate them in competing models without authorization. The company says these campaigns, often run through networks of fraudulent accounts, specifically target Claude's visible reasoning transcripts, which are the most valuable training signal for replication. The defenses operate in three layers. First, "preserved thinking" blocks new accounts from editing prior reasoning context, and reasoning transcripts are only visible to the requesting organization. Second, a two-stage classifier — red-teamed both internally and externally — detects extraction attempts with a false positive rate below 0.1%. Third, stricter rate limits, account bans, and charges on blocked requests deter repeat attempts. Results were significant: reasoning tokens extracted by distillers fell 10 to 100-fold, and tens of thousands of accounts were banned, often within two hours of their first request. Anthropic named Zhipu's GLM-5.3 as an example of a distilled model that a U.S. government assessment found to be the most capable open-weights cyber-attack tool released to date. Anthropic acknowledged trade-offs: legitimate developers occasionally need to update integrations, a small fraction of benign requests are misclassified, and blocked requests now incur charges. The company called for all frontier model providers and cloud platforms to share responsibility for distillation defense.
