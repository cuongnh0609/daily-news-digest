B3Vercelの「インポート候補」メールの条件を61リポジトリで徹底検証——正規表現でJSON文字列検索が判明

📰 本文 + 和越対訳

開発者向けデプロイプラットフォームVercelが、リポジトリ作成時に自動送信する「インポートできます」メールの判定ロジックを、61個のテストリポジトリを使って徹底的に検証した記事がZennに投稿された。調査の結果、メールの送信トリガーはリポジトリへのpushイベントであり、平均4.9秒という極めて短い時間で到着することが分かった。最も興味深い発見は判定方法だ。`package.json`やPythonマニフェストなどにフレームワーク依存関係の名前が含まれているかどうかを、JSONとして正しくパースするのではなく、JSON文字列に対して正規表現で直接検索しているという。そのため、JSONとして無効な形式のファイルや存在しないバージョン番号が記載されていてもメールが送信された。またディレクトリの深さに制限がなく、6階層下のファイルまで検出されたことも確認された。著者はこの実装が「ビルド可能性を事前確認せずに通知を出している」点や、GitHub Appが与えられた権限の本来の目的外に使われている点に倫理的な問題を指摘し、プライバシーリスクへの対処として「Selected repositories」設定への切り替えを推奨している。

Một bài viết trên Zenn đã công bố kết quả điều tra chi tiết về điều kiện kích hoạt email "Có thể import được" mà nền tảng deploy Vercel tự động gửi khi tạo repository, sử dụng 61 repository thử nghiệm. Kết quả cho thấy email được kích hoạt bởi sự kiện push lên repository và đến trong thời gian cực kỳ ngắn, trung bình 4.9 giây. Phát hiện thú vị nhất là về phương pháp phán đoán: thay vì parse JSON đúng cách, Vercel dùng regex để tìm kiếm trực tiếp trong chuỗi JSON xem có chứa tên dependency của framework hay không. Do đó, ngay cả file có định dạng JSON không hợp lệ hay số phiên bản không tồn tại cũng kích hoạt email. Ngoài ra còn xác nhận không có giới hạn độ sâu thư mục, với file ở 6 cấp sâu cũng được phát hiện. Tác giả chỉ ra vấn đề đạo đức khi implementation này "gửi thông báo mà không kiểm tra khả năng build trước", cũng như GitHub App đang được dùng ngoài mục đích ban đầu của quyền được cấp, và khuyến nghị chuyển sang cài đặt "Selected repositories" để phòng ngừa rủi ro quyền riêng tư.

C1Google Releases Android Bench 2.0 — AI Agents Scored on Week-Long Dev Tasks, Best Model Hits Only 28%

📰 Article (English)

Google has published Android Bench 2.0, a substantially revised benchmark designed to measure how well AI agents handle "one-week-equivalent" software development tasks — a far more realistic challenge than prior single-session evaluations. The original Android Bench saw leading models achieve up to 91% on its tasks, but the new version's multi-task, multi-session structure drove even top performers down to a 28% completion rate, with OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra leading the field, followed by Google's Gemini 3.8 Flash and Anthropic's Fable 5.1. Android Bench 2.0 introduces a "completion rate" metric that factors in task success, UI interaction accuracy, and recovery behavior when errors occur. It also adjusts scoring based on how much developer-interaction time the agent consumes, penalizing models that demand constant hand-holding. The benchmark covers realistic scenarios including multi-file refactoring, debugging across test suites, and coordinating tool calls over extended sessions. The stark drop from 91% to 28% signals that current AI agents still struggle enormously with context retention and autonomous decision-making over long time horizons — exactly the capability gap that matters most for real-world agentic engineering use cases.

C2Anthropic Launches Life Sciences Verification Program — Tiered Access for Biology Researchers with Offline Monitoring

📰 Article (English)

Anthropic has introduced the Life Sciences Verification Program (LSVP), a tiered access framework granting vetted biology professionals expanded capabilities in Claude with purpose-built safeguards. The program addresses a longstanding tension in AI safety: frontier models are powerful enough to accelerate legitimate research in drug discovery, clinical development, and manufacturing — but blanket restrictions that prevent misuse also block genuine scientists. LSVP offers two grant tiers. Standard Use grants cover broad daily research tasks under normal operational guardrails. High-risk Use grants remove additional safeguards for specific, pre-approved research projects that undergo renewed vetting every six months. Crucially, the program does not use real-time request blocking; instead it relies on offline session monitoring with 30-day data retention to detect misuse patterns after the fact, strictly compartmentalized away from model training. Access is available via the Claude API, Claude Science platform, and enterprise deployments. The security model explicitly addresses three threat scenarios: account compromise, insider threats, and unintended agent actions — the last of which is particularly relevant given recent incidents of AI agents acting beyond their authorized scope.

C3Meta Muse: First Consumer-Accessible Agentic AI — Gruber Praises the Engineering, Worries About User Awareness

📰 Article (English)

Meta's Muse has drawn significant attention as what commentators are calling "the first consumer-accessible agentic AI system" — one that gives users a persistent Linux virtual machine running in Meta's cloud infrastructure, with an AI that can autonomously operate it on their behalf. John Gruber of Daring Fireball praised Muse as "both groundbreaking technically" and "packaged in an easy-to-install, easy-to-use way," and Simon Willison amplified the observation with concern. The core capability is qualitatively different from previous AI assistants: rather than answering questions or generating text, Muse can spawn processes, modify files, make network requests, and take sequences of actions over time without returning to the user for confirmation at each step. It runs on dedicated infrastructure separate from the user's own machine, which provides a degree of isolation — but also means the AI is acting with real-world consequences in a persistent environment. Gruber's concern, quoted and echoed by Willison, is the power-tool analogy: consumers buying a table saw generally understand they're buying something dangerous. Muse users may not grasp that they're deploying an autonomous agent with real computational resources. The design prioritizes accessibility over explicit capability disclosure — raising questions about consent, accountability, and what happens when agents act beyond what users intended.
