EEBench is a new benchmark designed to objectively measure whether AI models can design functional electronic circuits — moving AI evaluation beyond software into physical hardware engineering. Rather than using graphical CAD tools, EEBench leverages atopile, a declarative code-based approach to circuit design where models work directly with electrical components and constraints. The benchmark goes beyond theoretical design. It runs SPICE simulations across multiple tolerance corners, accounting for real-world component variation — for example, ceramic capacitors may deliver significantly less capacitance than their rated value under operating voltage bias. One failing design used a 22 µF capacitor that effectively delivered only 11.4 µF at operating conditions, missing the circuit's 545 µF requirement. The grading system evaluates cost, supply availability, and practical part selection, closely mirroring real engineering trade-offs. The September 2026 leaderboard: Claude Opus 5 leads at 61.6%, followed by Grok 4.6 at 57.1%, and Claude Fable 5.1 at 56.4%. Notably, xAI published Grok 4.6's EEBench results in official model documentation, suggesting the industry is starting to take this benchmark seriously. The authors conclude that AI can already tackle "a useful and growing set of circuit problems" — but explicitly caution they would not trust AI-designed medical devices without additional human verification.
