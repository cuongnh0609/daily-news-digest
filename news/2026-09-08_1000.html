Anthropic has launched a research preview of the Model Hardware Standard (MHS), an open specification that allows AI agents to safely operate physical laboratory and industrial devices. Analogous to a device driver that bridges an operating system and hardware, MHS provides a standardized interface using simple "read" and "write" primitives so that any compatible agent can discover and control unfamiliar equipment without bespoke translation layers. Drivers written to the MHS spec contain plain-language tags that auto-generate reference documents describing device capabilities, safety limits, and operating parameters — enabling Claude and future agents to reason about equipment they have never encountered before. Control can occur via MCP, CLI, or API, and a single line of code can orchestrate multiple devices simultaneously. Early production results are striking: at Genentech, Claude autonomously optimized flow rates across liquid handlers and robotic arms; at the University of Washington, manual intervention in qPCR workflows dropped significantly; at QuEra (quantum computing), laser frequency recovery success improved from 58% to 99.3% while recovery time shrank from 150 seconds to 6 seconds. Carnegie Mellon reduced dose-response experiment time by roughly 66%. The spec is model-agnostic. A research preview is accepting applications at modelhardwarestandard.com, with open-source release planned after safety evaluation. Anthropic acknowledges current limitations: Claude lacks strong spatial reasoning and physical intuition for edge-case troubleshooting, and non-programmable hardware remains unsupported.
