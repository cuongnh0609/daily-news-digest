A5AIの台頭でウェブ集客はSEOからGEOへ――生成AI検索、初めて利用率5割超え

📰 本文 + 和越対訳

生成AI検索の普及に伴い、企業のウェブ集客戦略が従来のSEO（検索エンジン最適化）からGEO（生成エンジン最適化）へとシフトを迫られている。サイバーエージェントのリサーチ部門「GEO Lab」の調査によると、2026年8月の生成AI検索の利用率は52.3%と、初めて半数を超えた（2025年5月は21.3%）。従来型キーワード検索は86.9%とまだ主流だが、前年比で低下が続く。この変化がもたらす最大の問題は「ゼロクリック」現象だ。生成AIがサイトを訪れることなく質問に答えてしまうため、ウェブサイトへのアクセス自体が激減する。Chartbeatのデータでは、2025年7月から2026年7月の1年間でGoogleからの流入が約40%減少した。企業がGEOに対応するには、キーワード選定だけでは不十分で、AIに「信頼できる情報源」と認識してもらう必要がある。具体的には、独自調査データの公開、専門家コメントの引用、定義から始まる構造的な記述が有効とされる。今後は「AIに何回引用されたか」がPV数に代わる新たな成果指標になるとの見方も出ている。

Làn sóng tìm kiếm bằng AI tạo sinh đang buộc các doanh nghiệp phải chuyển chiến lược web marketing từ SEO (tối ưu hóa cho công cụ tìm kiếm truyền thống) sang GEO (Generative Engine Optimization). Theo khảo sát của GEO Lab thuộc CyberAgent, tỷ lệ sử dụng AI search đã vượt 52,3% vào tháng 8/2026 — lần đầu tiên vượt ngưỡng 50%, so với chỉ 21,3% hồi tháng 5/2025. Vấn đề lớn nhất là hiện tượng "zero-click": AI trả lời câu hỏi trực tiếp mà không cần người dùng vào website. Dữ liệu Chartbeat cho thấy lượng truy cập từ Google giảm khoảng 40% trong 1 năm qua. Để thích nghi với GEO, doanh nghiệp cần được AI công nhận là nguồn đáng tin cậy bằng cách công bố dữ liệu độc quyền, trích dẫn chuyên gia và viết nội dung có cấu trúc rõ ràng. Trong tương lai, "số lần được AI trích dẫn" có thể thay thế lượt xem trang như chỉ số thành công mới.

C2Google Quantum AI Demonstrates Magic State Cultivation on Hardware — Error Rate Cut 40× on Willow Chip

📰 Article (English)

Google Quantum AI has demonstrated "magic state cultivation" on real quantum hardware for the first time, publishing results in the journal Science on October 15. Magic states are special quantum states required to execute fault-tolerant operations — a prerequisite for quantum computers to outperform classical ones on practically useful tasks. The problem is that generating high-quality magic states is extraordinarily expensive; some estimates put the overhead at up to 95% of a quantum computer's total capacity, and because each magic state is consumed after a single use, they must be produced continuously during computation. Researchers have worked on reducing this cost for approximately 20 years. The conventional approach, called magic state distillation, requires many physical qubits and significant time. Google's cultivation method, proposed theoretically in 2024, injects a noisy magic state into a small region of its Willow processor, repeatedly checks for errors, and discards failed attempts. This filtering process gradually produces high-fidelity states. On hardware, cultivation reduced the error rate to roughly one-fortieth of the baseline, with cultivated states reaching ≥99.99% fidelity — a new record. Only about 8% of attempts passed error checks, but those that did were of extremely high quality. The team also demonstrated "grafting" of states between error correction code families, bridging the color code used for cultivation and the surface code suited to larger-scale computation. This is a key step toward integrating cultivation into Google's roadmap for a practical, error-corrected quantum computer. Larger demonstrations remain ahead before practical use.

C3If You Don't Run Your Own LLM Proxy, How Do You Know What's Going On?

📰 Article (English)

Jared Siegel argues that developers who rely solely on provider frontends and analytics dashboards lack the engineering-grade visibility needed to manage LLM usage effectively. Provider analytics panels, he writes, show "proof of work" (decorative outputs) rather than actionable data, and simply exporting OpenTelemetry traces is not a sufficient answer. Running your own LLM proxy gives you: a single controlled endpoint for multiple providers and models; logs showing which API key called which model, from which client, at what time, and with what status code; virtual API keys that keep your primary key from being scattered across work machines, agent harnesses, and personal projects; infrastructure you own and can modify; and the ability to swap providers or models without reconfiguring every client. Siegel does not recommend a specific proxy product. He self-hosts a modified proxy at home for learning and at work because he treats instrumentation as part of doing the job properly. His example: he can pinpoint the cause of a cache miss during a coding session in about 20 seconds by hand, and a proxy API could automate that entirely. The core thesis is ownership: "Own your infrastructure, your application visibility, data pathing, and storage." As more teams run agents, coding assistants, and MCP servers simultaneously, the argument for a centralized observability layer becomes difficult to dismiss. Without it, debugging latency spikes, unexpected token usage, or model behavior differences across providers is essentially guesswork.
