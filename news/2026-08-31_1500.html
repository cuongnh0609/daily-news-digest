📰 Article (English)

Continuous Diffusion Language Models (CDLMs) — largely abandoned by 2023 — are experiencing a significant research renaissance in 2025–2026, with recent papers claiming they now scale competitively with discrete autoregressive models. Unlike standard LLMs that generate text sequentially one token at a time, CDLMs embed tokens as continuous vectors, apply Gaussian noise, and then learn to reverse that corruption process. This continuous formulation gives CDLMs access to the full repertoire of diffusion techniques developed for images and audio, enabling parallel generation rather than sequential decoding. The key technical breakthrough enabling the revival is distillability: flow map methods allow step distillation that captures token correlations even in single-step sampling — a feat discrete diffusion cannot achieve due to its conditional independence assumptions. Several critical ingredients distinguish modern CDLMs: self-conditioning (nearly universal now, providing the largest performance boost), carefully designed loss functions balancing MSE and cross-entropy, adaptive noise schedules tuned to embedding geometry, and pre-trained embedding initializations. The author emphasizes that self-conditioning "tends to provide a huge boost in performance" and is now treated as a near-mandatory component. The 2025–2026 revival also reflects a shift in evaluation philosophy: the community has moved beyond purely efficiency-focused benchmarks, creating space to appreciate CDLMs' unique properties, including better controllability and the potential for non-left-to-right generation.
