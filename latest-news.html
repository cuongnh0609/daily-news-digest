C-03Agent Swarm Attempts to Solve P vs. NP — 47 Specialized Agents, 11 Days, Zero Proof

📰 Original Article (EN)

A research team published results of a 11-day experiment in which 47 specialized LLM-based agents collaborated in an attempt to solve the P vs. NP problem — one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems with a $1 million prize. The swarm was organized using a hierarchical orchestration model: a Coordinator agent dispatched tasks to specialized subagents covering complexity theory, proof verification, SAT solver analysis, and literature synthesis. Each subagent operated in an isolated sandbox with tool access to formal proof systems (Lean 4, Coq), academic paper search, and code execution. After 11 days and over 400,000 agent-to-agent messages, the swarm produced several novel partial results including three new conditional proofs, but did not resolve the central question. The experiment is notable for demonstrating the limits and potential of multi-agent systems on hard unsolved problems, the importance of agent memory and context handoff at scale, and failure modes unique to agentic coordination (circular reasoning loops, context poisoning across agents).
