C-2Debian Votes on LLM Usage — Four Competing Proposals from Full Ban to Open Acceptance

📰 Original (EN)

The Debian project, one of the oldest and most influential Linux distributions, opened a formal General Resolution vote on July 24, 2026 to decide its official stance on LLM-assisted contributions. Four competing proposals are on the table. Proposal A calls for a full ban — prohibiting all LLM-assisted contributions to packages, documentation, and web resources, citing concerns about copyright clarity, code quality, community impact, and ethics. Proposal B allows AI assistance with conditions: contributors must ensure legal compatibility, verify licensing, disclose tool use, maintain accountability, and avoid sending sensitive data to untrusted providers. Proposal C discourages LLM use while requiring disclosure, and restricts AI assistance in human communications. Proposal D takes the most permissive stance: accepting AI-assisted work for Debian-specific projects while placing full responsibility on submitters to verify compliance and mark AI usage appropriately. The outcome of this vote will set a precedent for how major open-source ecosystems handle LLM contributions — with implications extending far beyond Debian itself.

A new open-weight text-to-speech model called Inflect-Micro-v2 has been released on Hugging Face, delivering complete local text-to-waveform speech synthesis at 24 kHz quality using just 9.36 million parameters and 37.53 MB of storage. Built on the VITS architecture with 192 latent channels and a 3-layer encoder, the model synthesizes speech at 6.28× faster than real-time on standard CPU hardware without requiring a GPU. Released under Apache 2.0 license, it represents a significant advance in making high-quality speech synthesis practical for edge devices, embedded systems, and privacy-sensitive local deployments where sending audio to cloud APIs is not acceptable.
