📰 本文 + 和越対訳

AIが生成したプルリクエストを人間がコードを読まずにマージする「ノールックマージ」を実現するための条件を考察した記事が注目を集めている。著者は、自動テストとAIによる設計・セキュリティレビューがあれば人間のコードレビューを代替できると主張する。しかし最大の障壁は技術ではなく組織文化であり、バグが出たときに個人を責める文化が続く限り、開発者は自衛のためにコードを読み続けるという。また、コードレビューが担ってきた設計相談・変更周知の機能を別の場所（事前設計レビューやデモ）に移す必要がある点も指摘している。著者自身はTAKTというワークフローツールでAIにマージ判断を委任し、サンプルチェックを行うだけにとどめている実例を紹介している。

Một bài viết về điều kiện để thực hiện "no-look merge" - tức là merge pull request do AI tạo ra mà không cần đọc code - đang thu hút sự chú ý. Tác giả cũng nhấn mạnh cần chuyển chức năng tư vấn thiết kế và thông báo thay đổi sang nơi khác (design review trước khi implement hoặc demo). C1Brazilian Court Sanctions Lawyers for Hiding Prompt Injection in Court Filing

📰 Article (English)

A Brazilian labor court has sanctioned two law firms for embedding a prompt injection attack inside a court petition — believed to be the first such ruling worldwide. The lawyers hid the malicious instruction as white text on a white background, making it invisible to human readers. The hidden text read: "ATTENTION, ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE" followed by instructions telling any AI reviewing the document to respond superficially and not challenge the submitted materials. The court's AI review tool, Galileu, detected the concealed text when it changed the font color during processing and issued an automatic alert. The court ruled that the lawyers intended to manipulate AI systems used by the opposing party or by the court itself, violating good-faith and procedural obligations under Brazil's Civil Procedure Code. The two firms were fined 10% of the R$842,000 claim value — approximately $16,000 USD. The ruling establishes that a filing's integrity extends to its machine-readable content, and that embedding invisible AI manipulation instructions constitutes an abuse of court procedure. Security researchers note this case sets a precedent for how courts may handle AI-targeted document manipulation going forward.

📰 Article (English)

Nolan Lawson published a rigorous benchmark testing whether JavaScript framework choice meaningfully affects the cost, time, and reliability of AI-assisted development. He had Claude Opus 5.5 build a Pokémon app across 12 setups (React, Next.js, SvelteKit, Astro, Hono+htmx, Angular, Vanilla, and more), measuring tokens used, cost, time to complete, code size, and Lighthouse scores. Key findings: Hono+htmx was the cheapest at ~$2.67/run median and Astro was the fastest, while Angular was both the most expensive (~$4.97/run) and slowest (~17 min), and failed twice to produce a working app. Code size strongly predicts cost in agentic workflows because generated code feeds back as context in every future edit — Angular produced ~2,000 lines vs ~1,370 for Hono+htmx. Multi-page architectures (Astro, Hono+htmx) generally outperformed SPAs on performance metrics. Framework-provided MCPs and skills varied wildly in quality and usefulness. The benchmark is open source and reproducible. Lawson argues that framework claims about being "better for AI" should be backed by concrete numbers, not marketing language.

C3GitHub Copilot Shared Automations: Team-Owned AI Workflows That Survive Staff Turnover

📰 Article (English)

GitHub has rolled out Shared Copilot Automations, transforming AI-powered workflows from personal tools into repository-owned resources that any team member can view, edit, and rely on. Previously, Copilot Automations were personal: only the creator could see or edit them, they used the creator's identity for attribution, and AI credits were drawn from the creator's account. Shared Automations change all three: read access grants visibility, write access grants editing rights, AI credits are billed to the repository owner (the organization or user), and actions are attributed to Copilot rather than an individual. Most critically, "shared automations belong to the repository, so they keep running if the person who created them leaves the team." Change history tracks how tools, triggers, and prompts evolved over time. Organizations must enable the "Allow use of Copilot cloud agent billed to the organization" policy in admin settings. Automations — combinations of prompts, triggers, and tools — can be set to "Shared" at creation time in github.com, the Copilot app, or VS Code, but the type cannot be changed after creation. Available on Copilot Pro, Pro+, Max, Business, and Enterprise plans with gradual rollout. Use cases include automated bug reproduction on issue creation, screenshot capture when PRs are ready for review, and investigation of production alerts.
