C1Astra Can Read and Write Neural Network Weights Directly — Weight-Space Programming Emerges

📰 Article (English)

Researchers at UC Berkeley and Tel Aviv University published a striking finding: GPT-6 Astra, used as a general-purpose LLM, can reason about and directly manipulate neural network weights without any gradient descent or optimizer. They term this capability "weight-space programming." In the reading direction, Astra identified the purpose of four reinforcement-learning policies purely from raw weight values — inferring which game each was trained for, whether a language model was sycophantic, and how diffusion model outputs were shaped. In the writing direction, Astra edited 3.5% of the weights of a failing car-control policy and made it succeed in all 1,024 test episodes. Most dramatically, it edited 131,072 weights of Qwen3.5-0.8B (0.016% of its total) to raise MATH-500 accuracy from 7.9% to 48.1%, by tracing a reasoning loop to a specific direction in two MLP layers and removing it. The authors report that Astra could also write game-playing policies for MinAtar and sketch a 1.5M-parameter Go player using only Python and a handful of forward passes. The paper openly acknowledges limits: Astra could not improve Gemma 3 270M and could not locate the sycophancy direction without attention-pattern access.

📰 Article (English)

Fields Medal winner Terence Tao hosted a guest essay on his blog by researchers Karthik C. S., Amit Sahai, and Jie Xue, arguing that AI's rise in mathematics should push practitioners to question what actually gives their work value. The essay takes a Platonist stance — mathematical truths exist independently of us — which means discovery takes creativity but the truths are not personal possessions. The authors argue that metrics like publications, citations, and awards are a pre-AI problem that merely becomes acute now: when a mathematician's worth is tied to outputs, AI looks like a direct competitor. Their proposed corrective is "egoless mathematics" — letting truth, rather than one's standing, be the primary motivation. They note that mathematical understanding, unlike a scarce resource, is not diminished when others share it. The practical implication is that mathematicians who embrace AI tools and those who avoid them can share the same ethical commitment to truth. The essay stops short of prescribing policy and admits the authors have not fully achieved the egoless ideal themselves. It arrives in the context of LLMs increasingly passing graduate-level math benchmarks and assisting in formal proof verification — areas where the human–AI boundary is actively shifting.
