C-03What Would a Serious AI Product Look Like? — Seven Features Missing From Every Major Vendor

📰 Article (English)

Glyph (Bryant Moseley) argues that current AI products are fundamentally unserious — not because the models are bad, but because the product layer systematically discourages verification and obscures the model's limitations behind legal disclaimers and vague disclaimers. A "serious" product, he contends, would be built around catching and correcting errors, not hiding them. He proposes seven concrete features: (1) inline checkboxes for verified claims rather than buried fine-print disclaimers; (2) prominent citations with full metadata, not invisible footnote links; (3) minimal verbosity — no apologetic filler phrases; (4) task-specific interfaces instead of general-purpose chat boxes; (5) data provenance tracking distinguishing API results from model hallucinations; (6) reproducibility controls exposing temperature and enabling session replay; (7) visible context windows showing what the model is actually working with. For coding tools specifically, he adds sandboxed execution with enforced rollbacks as a baseline requirement, not an optional add-on. The broader argument is that absent these features, the current generation of AI products provides value only for tasks where verification doesn't matter — and when you account for the hidden externalities of unverified AI output being acted upon at scale, vendors' lack of interest in building these features becomes a structural problem.
