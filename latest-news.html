C2Google Research Proposes SLEEP Framework for LLM Continual Learning — Near-Zero Forgetting

📰 Article (English)

Google Research published the SLEEP framework — a new approach to continual learning in large language models that alternates between a "wake" phase and a "sleep" phase, drawing loose inspiration from biological sleep cycles. During the wake phase, the model performs normal inference while accumulating short-term memory. The sleep phase then has two components: memory consolidation (using low-rank mixture-of-experts parameter expansion, knowledge distillation, and RL-based imitation) and "dreaming" (random expert activation generating synthetic data, filtered by gradient-based selection and RL refinement). In benchmarks, SLEEP achieves near-zero forgetting on continual translation tasks, near-perfect scaling to 10 million tokens on BABILong, and strong performance on AIME 24/25 and HMMT 25 math competitions — all surpassing SFT, GRPO, and ICL baselines. The key insight is separating active learning from weight consolidation, preventing catastrophic forgetting without requiring task boundaries or replay buffers. Full paper: arXiv 2606.03979.
