C-03Three Critical Gaps in AI Long-Term Memory: A Developer's Survey of mem0, Letta, and the Research Literature

📰 Article (English)

A developer published a comprehensive survey of existing approaches to AI long-term memory — covering commercial products like mem0 and Letta, open-source projects, and academic research — identifying three critical gaps that no existing solution fully addresses. Gap 1: Design convergence without long-term operational data. Every approach converged on the same pattern: extract insights from conversations, store as Markdown files, inject a table of contents at session start. Yet nobody published reports on successfully running these systems beyond initial implementation. Construction guides are abundant; operational post-mortems are entirely absent. Gap 2: Product-specific limitations. mem0 inadvertently deletes correct memories when updating related facts. Letta's benchmarks showed that plain file storage with text search outperformed sophisticated graph-based memory at 74.0% vs 68.5%. Zep/Graphiti showed the most mature approach by timestamping facts with validity periods rather than overwriting. Gap 3: Research shift from extraction to contamination. Academic focus moved from "can we extract knowledge?" to "how do we prevent extracted knowledge from corrupting subsequent cycles?" Errors propagate irreversibly across self-distillation iterations. The author built a system that: preserves raw records alongside distilled notes, marks outdated information rather than deleting it, gates human review on what gets stored, and maintains temporal metadata for future queries.
