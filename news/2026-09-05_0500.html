Bài viết cũng chỉ ra vấn đề cốt lõi: agent không có log thì không thể dừng lại, và observability (khả năng quan sát) là điều kiện bắt buộc để quản trị agent. C-01GitHub Project HydraFusion: Multi-Model Orchestration Cuts AI Coding Costs by 65% at Near-Frontier Quality

📰 Article (English)

GitHub has published research on Project HydraFusion, a Copilot research preview that dynamically selects and orchestrates multiple AI models at runtime — eliminating the need for developers to manually choose a model upfront. Instead of routing every request to a single frontier model, HydraFusion treats workflow selection as an optimization problem and uses three execution patterns: Single (one model solves directly), Cascade (efficient model drafts, quality gate decides whether to escalate), and Critique (model A drafts, independent model B critiques, A revises). Five design principles govern the system: complete cost accounting, bounded execution with timeouts, isolated review (reviewer works tool-less while solver uses shared workspace), fail-safe to prevent incomplete commits, and validated routing before execution. Benchmarks against Claude Opus 5 show impressive results: 67% cost reduction on TerminalBench 2.1 with +4.9pp quality gain, 65% reduction on CheckpointBench with near-identical quality, and 36% reduction on DeepSWE at -1.5pp quality. The system is currently available in GitHub Copilot CLI's /experimental channel. HydraFusion represents a practical realization of multi-agent orchestration patterns moving from research into production developer tooling.

🔴 HIGH Multi-model orchestration as default coding workflow — significant cost and quality implications for Copilot users and teams.

C-02Anthropic AI Agents Formally Verify Fermat's Last Theorem in 11 Days — 13 Million Lines of Lean Code

📰 Article (English)

Anthropic researchers have achieved a landmark milestone in AI-assisted mathematics: the first complete computer-verified proof of Fermat's Last Theorem, accomplished in just 11 days using Claude agents working largely autonomously through a multi-agent platform called Prove2Me. The formalization produced 13 million lines of Lean code — a formal proof assistant language — and required proving 29,500 intermediate theorems along the way. The resulting codebase is 5× larger than Mathlib, the primary community mathematics library. The project consumed approximately six billion output tokens from an internal research model comparable to Claude Fable 5.1. Prove2Me maintained a directed acyclic graph (DAG) tracking all theorem dependencies, enabling multiple agents to work in parallel without losing project context. It also streamlined compilation and facilitated theorem discovery through natural language descriptions. The significance is twofold: it demonstrates AI can automate large-scale formal mathematics at a scale and speed humans cannot match, and opens the door to systematic formalization of the broader mathematical literature. Mathematician Kevin Buzzard noted the system could help catch errors in established mathematical proofs. This work signals that formal verification may become a standard layer alongside human-readable proofs for AI-generated mathematics.
