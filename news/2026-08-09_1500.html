C-01Shopify Replaced Redis with MySQL for Inventory Reservations — Scaling to $5.1M Sales Per Minute

📰 Article (English)

Shopify's engineering blog revealed how the company replaced Redis with MySQL 8 for handling inventory reservations during checkout, enabling the platform to scale to $5.1 million in sales per minute during peak flash sales. The core innovation was adopting MySQL 8's SKIP LOCKED feature combined with a fundamentally different data model: instead of "one row per item with a quantity column," they implemented "one row per sellable unit," capping pools at 1,000 rows per item-location pair. This design allows truly concurrent transactions to reserve units without contention or queue bottlenecks. Three additional optimizations drove performance: restructuring composite primary keys to include filter columns reduced lock counts from two to one per row; switching to READ COMMITTED isolation eliminated gap locks that blocked replenishment operations; and standardized transaction sequences (lock ordering) prevented deadlocks between reserve and claim operations. Batching with UNION ALL consolidated multi-item cart reservations into single database round trips. Surprisingly, the real bottleneck wasn't query performance but connection pool exhaustion. Engineers added per-caller attribution tags in SQL comments and tracked connection hold times at the proxy layer, discovering unrelated checkout processes consuming excessive connections. Optimizing those outliers and tuning InnoDB thread concurrency removed the final scaling ceiling entirely.
