A-05eMudhra認証局の中間証明書に秘密鍵の致命的脆弱性——大手CAが緊急失効処理

📰 本文 + 和越対訳

インドの認証局（CA）であるeMudhraが発行した中間証明書に、秘密鍵が「数ミリ秒」で復元可能な致命的な脆弱性が確認された。研究者がリバースエンジニアリングによって脆弱性を発見し、証明書の信頼性が根本から損なわれていることが明らかになった。これを受け、GlobalSignやDigiCertをはじめとする主要な認証局は緊急対応として当該中間証明書を失効リストに登録し、信頼チェーンから切り離す措置を取った。影響範囲はeMudhra傘下の多数のドメイン証明書に及ぶ可能性があり、ITシステム管理者は自社の証明書状況を早急に確認するよう求められている。WebPKI（公開鍵基盤）の信頼性を揺るがしかねない重大事案として、セキュリティコミュニティで広く議論されている。

Cơ quan cấp chứng chỉ (CA) eMudhra của Ấn Độ có lỗ hổng nghiêm trọng trong chứng chỉ trung gian, cho phép khôi phục khóa bí mật (private key) chỉ trong "vài mili giây". Nhà nghiên cứu phát hiện lỗ hổng qua kỹ thuật reverse engineering, cho thấy tính toàn vẹn của chứng chỉ bị xâm phạm hoàn toàn. GlobalSign, DigiCert và các CA lớn khác đã lập tức thu hồi (revoke) các chứng chỉ trung gian liên quan và loại chúng khỏi chuỗi tin cậy. Quản trị viên IT được khuyến cáo kiểm tra ngay trạng thái chứng chỉ của hệ thống, vì sự cố này có thể ảnh hưởng đến nhiều domain được eMudhra cấp phát.

C-02Google Open-Sources TorchTPU: Native PyTorch Backend for TPUs via Apache 2.0

📰 Article (English)

Google has open-sourced TorchTPU under Apache 2.0, a native PyTorch backend that lets developers run existing PyTorch models directly on Google's Tensor Processing Units (TPUs) without switching to a separate framework. The project provides custom ATen kernels for standard PyTorch operations, a compilation cache that accelerates XLA graph execution on repeat runs, and native support for both data and tensor parallelism in distributed training scenarios. Integration with torch.compile() enables graph-mode execution, which the team found most effective for production workloads. The repository ships example implementations for Llama, Qwen, ResNet, DLRM, and minGPT, along with debugging and benchmarking utilities. Installation is via pip (pip install torchtpu, currently v0.2.0) with Python 3.11+ support, or from source via Bazel. The release lowers the barrier to using TPU hardware for teams already invested in the PyTorch ecosystem, where JAX and older torch_xla were previously the only paths to TPU acceleration. This is particularly relevant for organizations training large models who want hardware optionality beyond Nvidia GPUs.

C-03SF Compute: The Programmable Data Center — Why AI Compute Needs Physical Settlement and Multi-Attribute Order Books

📰 Article (English)

Evan Conrad of SF Compute published a detailed essay arguing that AI compute markets can only become liquid and trustworthy if contracts are physically settled rather than cash-settled against a benchmark index. The thesis draws from commodity trading: a compute exchange that only settles financially creates index-manipulation risk and divergence from actual market costs. SF Compute's approach is to operate the full data center stack — power distribution, switching, DPUs, fabric management, and BMCs — as a neutral venue independent of hardware vendors and hyperscalers. Their multi-attribute order book matches orders by specifying constraints (GPU type, memory, time window, interruptibility) rather than waiting for commodity SKU standardization. This generates liquidity even during rapid hardware generational change. Conrad distinguishes between interruptible batch inference capacity (tolerates interruption, priced lower) and live inference capacity (SLA cost, longer notice required). He also flags that early GPU hardware cohorts with firmware bugs can materially affect performance, requiring per-cohort contract terms. The programmable backplane lets customers subdivide InfiniBand fabrics on demand — a capability absent from most cloud providers.
